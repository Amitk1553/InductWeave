# Canada and United Kingdom Onboarding Policy

This policy applies to employees based in Toronto (Canada) and London (United Kingdom). It supplements the Global Onboarding Policy.

## Canada: Social Insurance Number

Every employee in Canada must provide a valid Social Insurance Number (SIN) to HR within three business days of starting. The SIN is required by law for payroll, income tax reporting and Canada Pension Plan contributions. Employees on a temporary work permit have a SIN that begins with the digit 9 and expires with the permit; HR tracks the expiry date and will contact you before it lapses. Please submit your SIN through the secure HR portal and never send it by email or chat.

## Canada: TD1 Forms

On or before your first day, complete the federal TD1 Personal Tax Credits Return and the Ontario TD1ON provincial form. These determine the amount of tax deducted from each paycheque. If you have no changes to claim, you can submit the basic personal amount only. Without TD1 forms, payroll deducts tax without personal credits. You may submit a new TD1 whenever your circumstances change.

## UK: Right to Work

UK law requires HR to check every new employee's right to work in the United Kingdom before they start working. Bring your original documents, such as a UK or Irish passport, or your share code for a digital check through the Home Office service, to HR before your start date; HR aims to complete the check one working day before you start. A copy is retained for the duration of your employment plus two years. If your right to work is time-limited, HR diarises a follow-up check before your permission expires. You cannot begin work until the check is complete.

## UK: P45 and National Insurance Number

Provide the P45 you received from your previous employer to payroll within three days of starting so that the correct tax code is applied. If you do not have a P45, HR will ask you to complete a starter checklist instead. You must also provide your National Insurance (NI) number. If you do not have one yet, apply through GOV.UK and share it with payroll as soon as you receive it; until then, tax is deducted on an emergency basis. Pay is issued on the last working day of the month.

## Statutory Leave (Canada and UK)

In the UK employees receive 28 days of paid holiday including public holidays, pro-rated in the first year, plus statutory sick pay after three waiting days, and statutory maternity, paternity and shared parental leave. In Ontario, employees receive 3 weeks of paid vacation after the first year, 10 days of job-protected personal emergency leave, and statutory maternity and parental leave as set by the Employment Standards Act; the company tops up pay for parental leave according to the benefits portal.

## Pension and Benefits

UK employees are automatically enrolled in the workplace pension after the first month, with an employer contribution of 5 percent. You can opt out within one month of enrollment. Private medical insurance is provided for employees in London and must be enrolled within 30 days of the start date. In Canada, group health and dental coverage starts after a 30-day waiting period, and a Registered Retirement Savings Plan with employer matching of up to 3 percent is available after three months.
